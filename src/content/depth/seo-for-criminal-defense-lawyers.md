## SEO for criminal defense lawyers is a race to the first call

A defense search is urgent in a way few searches are. It happens in the hours after an arrest, often at night, and often by a parent or partner rather than the person charged.

So a defense site writes for three readers: the person charged, the relative making calls for them, and the person who wants an old record cleared months later. Each one uses different words, and a relative rarely knows the charge's legal name.

[slot family]

The page that answers the jail, the bail and the first court date gets the relative's call. The record-clearing reader is the calm one, with time to compare, so that page can go deeper and keeps working all year.

[more "See how we find the searches that pay"]

For each search we target, we note who holds the top results in each county you serve, what kind of site each one is and which questions Google suggests. The questions no ranking page answers well become sections on your pages.

The rival a firm worries about is often not the one Google shows. On one injury account, the competitor we were told to watch didn't turn up once in the searches we tracked. So we pick rivals by how often they actually appear, not by reputation.

[/more]

## What we build for a defense firm, in order

Everything we have proved so far, we proved for injury firms, and the order of work transfers. What changes for defense work is the law each page leans on, the courts it names and what it may promise. Our [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/) page walks through the first 90 days.

[steps]
1. **The charges you want, and the ones you refer out.**
2. **Your state's criminal code, read and checked for changes.**
3. **A page for each courthouse your lawyers use.**
4. **A page for each charge you want more of.**
5. **A page for the family, and one for the first appearance.**

[more "See what goes into each step"]

1. First the lawyer tells us which cases the firm takes, which it sends elsewhere, and which words or claims must stay off the site for good. On one injury account those lines came after the pages were written, and some pages had to come down, so now they come first.
2. We read each statute itself, then check whether this session's bills changed it, since the official code page can lag behind a new act. Writers on one injury account had cited a repealed boating-under-the-influence statute that live law still pointed to.
3. A court page covers what a client needs for that court: where it sits, how the first hearing runs and what to bring. A page that names local courts names all of them, and one court list we inherited had skipped a whole level of appeal.
4. Each charge page uses the name your state's code gives the offense, and the everyday names people type go on that same page, not on pages of their own. Extra pages for each wording split the firm's strength.
5. The family page explains how to find someone in county custody, how bail works there and what happens next. It speaks to the caller and never asks them to describe the case in a web form.

[/more]

Towns without an office get no page from us, and a court page that would read just as true with the next county's name swapped in doesn't get published. How to write each of these pages is in our [criminal defense SEO guide](/criminal-defense-seo-guide/).

## After-hours calls, the Map and quiet reviews

Google answers a "near me" search with the Map, which leans toward the office nearest the searcher. A defense firm competes hardest around its own address, so its profile has to be complete and honest.

Honest starts with the hours. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) ask for the hours you really keep, so before new pages go live we settle who answers outside them:

[checklist]
- **A named person or service for nights and weekends,** or a callback promise with a stated time.
- **Profile hours that match that answer,** with holiday hours added.
- **A first question on every call:** is this the person charged, or someone calling for them?
- **The county and the next court date,** so callbacks go in order of urgency.
- **Web forms that reach a phone,** not an inbox read the next morning.

[more "See how we handle the profile and reviews"]

On the profile, each charge the firm takes becomes a service carrying the same name as its page. No category goes on for work the firm doesn't do. In our injury research we saw top-ranking firms in big cities break Google's rules and rank anyway, and we don't copy them.

Call tracking can live with the Map. The genuine number goes in the site's code and in every directory listing, and only visitors see the tracking number. On one injury account, a tracking number entered on the profile had been scraped by directories and listed as the firm's main line, exactly the mismatch this setup avoids.

Defense clients rarely want their name next to a case, so reviews arrive slowly. We ask everyone once the case is over, never just the satisfied ones ([Google's review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) bans that shortcut), and we live with a smaller count.

[/more]

## What may a defense page claim?

Defense marketing leans on "expert", "specialist" and dismissal headlines. Before we write a line, we read your state bar's own advertising rules, not a summary of them.

Most states follow [ABA Model Rule 7.2(c)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_2_advertising/): a lawyer may claim to be a certified specialist only when an approved body did the certifying, and the claim names that body. A dismissal or an acquittal is a past result, so your state's rules on results apply before it reaches a page.

[more "See how we check claims and credentials"]

We check every badge and credential where it was issued. On one injury account, a site listed a lawyer in an association he didn't belong to, and a bar admission date given from memory didn't match the official record.

We publish a result only if the lawyer can vouch for it. One lawyer we write for left a career total off his pages because he couldn't verify it, and a few specific results went up instead, each with a disclaimer.

No page calls the firm "the best" or promises an outcome, and the same rule holds for our own pages.

[/more]

Our own name uses "expert" about search, not law. Your firm, or its ethics counsel, signs off on every page before publication.

## When calls come, how we count them, and the cost

Give it about three months of groundwork first. Once a PI firm in New Orleans had it in place, its new truck pages were ranking within 16 hours of publication, yet nobody can promise a date for your county.

Visits don't count as results. Answered calls and qualified leads do, and for a defense firm a <dfn>qualified lead</dfn> is a call about a charge you take, from a court where you appear.
