## Who searches for an employment firm, and what do they type?

Two groups search for an employment firm: workers who describe what happened to them, and employers who describe a task or a risk. An employment practice ranks best when each group gets pages in its own words. A fired worker types what happened. An HR director types what to do next.

Workers search by problem, and by the kind of lawyer they think they need. These three searches come from our keyword research:

The first two sit close to a call: the worker has decided the problem is legal. The third comes earlier, from someone still employed.

Employers search by task: dismissing someone lawfully, answering a discrimination charge, reviewing a handbook.

General questions, such as what at-will employment means, bring traffic without calls.

How we build and rank both sets of pages each month is set out in our [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/).

### Should worker and employer pages share one site?

Worker and employer pages can share one site when each side has its own section, attorney bios, fee page and contact path. A separate brand makes sense only when the attorneys decide the two audiences shouldn't meet on one domain.

Either way, the worker side starts with issue pages.

## Which issue pages should an employment practice build first?

An employment practice should build first the issue pages for the claims it wants more of, each stating who it serves and the rule that applies. Those two facts screen out inquiries the firm can't take before anyone calls.

The table maps each issue page to its reader, its rule and the first question its form asks.

| Issue page | Who it is for | Rule to cite | First screening question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discrimination | Workers at a covered employer | EEOC: 15 or more employees; 180 or 300 days | How big is the employer? |
| Age discrimination | Workers treated worse because of age | EEOC: 20 or more employees for age claims | Which decision seemed to turn on age? |
| Harassment | Workers harassed on the job | EEOC: the deadline runs from the last incident | When was the last incident? |
| Retaliation | Workers punished after a complaint | EEOC charge window, or state law | What happened after you complained? |
| Equal pay | Workers paid less for equal work | Equal Pay Act: 2 years, 3 if willful | When was your last paycheck? |
| Unpaid wages and overtime | Workers owed pay | Federal or state wage law, per the agency | Which state do you work in? |
| Severance agreements | Workers handed an agreement to sign | The signing deadline in the agreement | Have you signed it yet? |
| Charge response (employer side) | Employers named in an EEOC charge | The EEOC's charge process | When did the charge notice arrive? |

The size thresholds come from the EEOC's page on [which private employers are covered](https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/coverage-businessprivate-employers), counted over at least 20 calendar weeks.

Each answer routes the reader to a consultation or to help elsewhere.

## Which filing deadlines should an employment firm publish?

An employment firm should publish every filing deadline its clients face, starting with the EEOC's 180-day charge window and its 300-day extension. A worker asking whether there is still time is close to calling.

The table lists deadlines that official sources state plainly, as of November 2026.

| Where | Claim | Time limit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| US, federal | Discrimination charge with the EEOC | 180 days, or 300 with a state or local agency | [EEOC time limits](https://www.eeoc.gov/time-limits-filing-charge) |
| US, federal | Harassment charge | 180 or 300 days from the last incident | EEOC time limits |
| US, federal | Lawsuit after a right-to-sue notice | 90 days from the notice | [EEOC: filing a lawsuit](https://www.eeoc.gov/filing-lawsuit) |
| US, federal | Equal Pay Act lawsuit | 2 years, or 3 if the violation was willful | EEOC: filing a lawsuit |
| England, Wales and Scotland | Most tribunal claims, problems from 1 October 2026 | 6 months minus 1 day | [Acas time limits](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-tribunal-time-limits) |
| England, Wales and Scotland | Most tribunal claims, problems before 1 October 2026 | 3 months minus 1 day | Acas time limits |

Small rules change the date. The EEOC counts weekends and holidays, and a deadline that lands on one moves to the next business day. In Britain, Acas early conciliation pauses the clock only if the worker contacts Acas within the time limit.

Each deadline page should state the limit in its first sentence, name the agency and link its page, and show the date it was checked. Google says its [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) need no special markup, only a page that is indexed and eligible for a snippet.

For a PI firm in El Paso, Google's AI answer cited the firm's Texas car accident filing deadline page as its first source. An employment deadline page is built the same way: one rule, stated plainly, with its source.

Every deadline page should tell the reader to confirm their own date with a lawyer. Employer-side pages cite the same rules for a different reader.

## Confidential settlements: what an employment firm can show instead

When settlements are confidential, an employment firm can show its process, attorney bios, verdicts it may publish and genuine reviews instead of case amounts. Each of those still earns trust and calls, inside the rules on what it may say.

In the US, each state sets lawyer advertising rules, and Texas shows the kind of limits involved.

Under Texas [Rule 7.01](https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/communications-concerning-a-lawyers-services/), a message misleads when it is substantially likely to raise unjustified expectations about what a lawyer can achieve. Under the same rule, a firm that advertises a verdict it knows was reduced must state the amount the client actually received, with equal or greater prominence.

Texas Ethics [Opinion 685](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-685/) (January 2020) lets a lawyer encourage clients to post reviews, provided nothing false, misleading or unfounded is encouraged.

Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) adds its own limits: no incentives, no asking only happy clients, no requested wording.

Never ask a settled client to name the employer or the case; the agreement may forbid it.

Since 21 October 2024, the FTC's [rule on reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) has barred fake reviews and review suppression. Reviews and testimonials from insiders, such as staff, must make their link to the business clear.

Outside the US, the rule source changes:

- England and Wales: paragraph 8.8 of the [SRA Code of Conduct](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/) requires publicity, charges included, to be accurate and not misleading.
- Canada: the CBA's [advertising toolkit](https://cba.org/resources/practice-tools/the-ethics-of-advertising-a-toolkit-for-lawyers/the-rules-of-advertising/) notes Ontario's commentary against words like "best", "super" and "#1".
- Australia: the Law Society of NSW quotes [rule 36.1](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/ethics-and-compliance/regulatory-compliance/advertising-legal-services) of the Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules, which in that state rules out advertising that is false, misleading, deceptive or offensive.

This is general information about marketing, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and country, so check yours with your bar, law society or ethics counsel.

## State law pages, city pages and the Map for employment work

Employment rights differ from state to state, so an employment practice gains more from a page per state law it handles than from a page per city. City pages that only swap the town name count as doorway pages under Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

State pages matter most below the federal size thresholds. An employer too small for federal law may still be covered by a state or local anti-discrimination law, the EEOC notes. A state page can name the agency that takes those complaints.

On the Map, each employment attorney can hold a practitioner profile beside the firm's own. Google's [business profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) rule out a separate profile for each specialty, so a "wage claims" profile isn't allowed.

Relevance, distance and prominence mainly decide local results, as Google's [local ranking guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) explains. An office ranks most easily for nearby workers; readers further away arrive through state law and issue pages.

Some of those readers will have cases the firm can't take, which is the first of the harder calls.

## Harder judgment calls for an employment practice

Three calls are harder than they look for an employment practice: turning cases away, serving clients outside the US, and judging the first few months.

### Turning away cases you can't take without losing the page

An employment firm can turn away cases it can't take and keep the page ranking by answering the question in full, then routing the reader out kindly. The page still earns the search, and the intake team stops spending calls on mismatches.

Three tools do the routing. A qualifying line near the top says which cases the firm takes, by issue, state and employer size. A short form asks the screening question from the issue table before a call is booked.

A referral paragraph says where else to go. For a worker with no lawyer, that can be the [EEOC Public Portal](https://www.eeoc.gov/how-file-charge-employment-discrimination), where people submit an inquiry and file a charge.

A worker still in the job may only be free after hours, so the form should offer a callback time.

Track which pages send the mismatches, then rewrite their qualifying lines. Our [personal injury CRM buyer's guide](/crm-software-for-personal-injury-lawyers/) shows how lead tracking ties each inquiry to its page, and the same setup works for employment intake.

### What changes for an employment firm in Britain, Australia or Canada

Employment firms in Britain face two rules that reach their pages: the longer tribunal limit and, for firms the SRA regulates in England and Wales, its price rules. The longer limit applies in England, Wales and Scotland, as the government's [employment changes guide](https://www.business.gov.uk/campaign/employment-changes/employers/employment-tribunal-time-limits/) confirms.

The [SRA Transparency Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/) require price and service information for employees' unfair or wrongful dismissal claims in the Employment Tribunal, and for defending those claims for employers. Each side then needs a price page with the total or range, the basis of charges, disbursements, VAT, key stages and who does the work. Use "solicitor" on these pages.

Canadian and Australian deadline pages should quote the time limit of the tribunal that hears the claim. Our work is remote, so firms outside the US can hire us. Fahim's results come from four countries, Ireland's Breakthrough Maths and New Zealand's Idiya among them.

### What the first months look like for an employment site

An employment site's first months go into groundwork: issue pages, deadline pages, the profiles and call tracking. That groundwork decides how fast later pages can move.

For the New Orleans PI firm, about three months of groundwork came before the tanker accident page in the hero ranked #3 within 16 hours of going live. Employment deadline pages can move the same way. No one can promise when, and rushing the groundwork is one way to lose good leads.

## Where employment law SEO loses good leads

Employment law SEO loses leads through review requests that name a case, undated deadline pages, one blended page for both sides, and claims that promise outcomes. Each costs trust with a worker or an employer, and some cost rankings too.

One agency page in this search calls informational content worthless now that Google shows AI answers. Google's own guidance says those features draw on indexed pages eligible for a snippet, and the El Paso deadline page above is one such page.

Other habits to refuse in a proposal: results from other practices shown as if they were employment wins, template copy reused across practice pages, and statistics with no primary source.

Done well, SEO for employment lawyers is dated, sourced and split by side, and it brings calls the firm can take.
