## How should an immigration practice map its visa types to each language?

An immigration practice should give each case type its own page, then build a separate version of that page in each language its lawyers and staff can serve. Google recommends a different URL for each language version, so Google can find and show each one.

Clients search for green card, citizenship or asylum help in English or in their first language, near your office or far from it. Three English searches from our keyword research show the pattern. Each names a case type: <q data-no-check>can a lawyer stop deportation</q>, <q data-no-check>can i apply for asylum without a lawyer</q> and <q data-no-check>how much does an immigration lawyer cost for green card</q>. Each deserves an answer in the client's own language.

Some searches look big and bring no clients. <q data-no-check>i-9 form</q> names an employer's work-eligibility form, a poor match for a firm that wants consults.

The table is an example plan for a firm whose attorneys speak English and Spanish. Your own case mix sets the rows.

| Case type page | What the page must name | Language versions |
|---|---|---|
| Removal defense | The immigration court the firm appears in | English and Spanish, each checked by a fluent attorney |
| Asylum | The official form and where its rules are published | English and Spanish |
| Family petitions | Who files, and where the relative waits | English and Spanish |
| Naturalization | The USCIS office where the interview happens | English and Spanish |
| Work visas (H-1B, PERM) | H-1B cap season dates and the PERM filing steps | English; Spanish if the firm advises employers in Spanish |
| Consults and fees | Price, length and languages of the first consult | Every language the firm serves |

A third language follows the same rule: build it only where someone at the firm can answer the phone in it.

### What Google asks of each language version

<dfn>Hreflang</dfn> is a tag that tells Google which pages are the same content in another language. Google's documentation makes six points about language versions, as of November 2026. The tag rule is a requirement and the translation limit is a spam policy; the other four are Google's recommendations:

[checklist]
- **One language per page.** Google reads the language from the [visible content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) and advises against side-by-side translations.
- **A separate URL for each version,** not a cookie or browser setting that swaps the text in place.
- **Links between versions,** which Google suggests so readers can pick their language.
- **No automatic redirect** based on a guessed language.
- **Reciprocal tags.** Each version lists itself and every other version; [one-way hreflang tags are ignored](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions). A plain "es" code is valid, while "es-419" is not supported.
- **No machine translation at scale.** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) treat pages made at scale through automated translating, with little added value, as scaled content abuse.

We add one rule of our own: the form, the confirmation and the first reply come in the page's language. Even then, a page earns trust only when it shows who stands behind the advice.

## How can an immigration firm's pages stand apart from notarios?

An immigration firm stands apart from notarios by proving, on every case page, that a licensed attorney does the legal work. [USCIS warns](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/common-scams) that a notario público has no authority to provide immigration legal services.

USCIS explains the confusion: in many Spanish-speaking countries, "notario" means a kind of attorney, but a US notary public is not authorized to provide legal services. The agency tells the public to check attorneys with the state bar, and accredited representatives on the Justice Department's roster.

So each case-type page makes that check easy. These five trust items go on every one:

[checklist]
- **The attorney's name and bar admission,** with a link to the state bar's lookup.
- **Who else the client will speak to,** with paralegals and interpreters named as such.
- **The first consult's price, length and languages,** stated plainly.
- **A Spanish page on notario versus abogado** that cites the USCIS warning.
- **No promise of approval or timing,** anywhere on the site.

These items help the reader who is already wary. They let a careful family tell a law firm from a notario before it calls. That check works the same whether the client lives nearby or in another state.

## If clients live in every state, where should an immigration firm rank?

An immigration firm can rank nationally with its case-type pages and locally with its Map listing. The federal definition in [8 CFR 1.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1.2) counts as an attorney anyone in good standing with the bar of the highest court of any US state, territory or DC.

So the client's state is not what limits a case-type page. Your own state bar's advertising rules still govern your site, and your counsel confirms how they apply.

The Map works differently. Under Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), each location gets one profile, virtual offices don't qualify, and service areas generally stay within about 2 hours' drive. A firm shows in the Map near its real offices, not across the country.

City pages belong where the firm has an office, attends an immigration court or meets clients. A page that swaps one city's name for another adds nothing a reader can use.

Reviews need care here. A review can reveal a client's status, so every client gets the same request and first names are welcome. Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) bars asking only happy clients, and our replies never confirm a case type.

If the current site can't hold language versions and office pages, our page on [law firm web design](/lawyer-web-design-development/) covers the rebuild. Wherever a page ranks, its claims must hold up under the rules that follow.

## What may an immigration firm's site claim under federal and state rules?

An immigration firm's site answers to its state bar's advertising rules and to federal rules for practitioners before the immigration courts, in [8 CFR 1003.102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1003.102). The federal rules bar claims about a practitioner's qualifications or services that cannot be substantiated.

The table sets out the federal points that most often change an immigration page or campaign.

| Rule | Where it sits | What it means for the firm |
|---|---|---|
| No unprovable claims | 8 CFR 1003.102(f) | Cut "fastest approvals" and similar lines |
| Specialist claims | 8 CFR 1003.102(f) | Only with certification a state authority grants or approves |
| Written solicitations | 8 CFR 1003.102(d) | Mail to new prospects carries "Advertising Material" on the envelope |
| Material at court | 8 CFR 1003.102(d) | No solicitation material in or around immigration court buildings |

Approval stories need the same care. A line like "green card approved in three months" implies a result the next client may not get, so it needs context, or it stays off the site.

## How fast should immigration pages change when policy changes?

An immigration page should change the week the rule it describes changes, and show the date it was last checked. USCIS's [January 30, 2026 bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCIS/bulletins/406da02), for one, set FY2027 H-1B registration for March 4 to 19, 2026, with weighted selection.

The same bulletin flagged a possible extra payment before filing, under a September 19, 2025 proclamation. A work-visa page written before that proclamation and never updated now leaves out a step its readers need.

We run each case-type page on four steps:

[steps]
1. **Name an owner.** One attorney checks each page and its language versions.
2. **Watch the source.** Track the official page each case page cites.
3. **Update within the week.** Change the page and its "last reviewed" date together.
4. **Move every language at once.** The Spanish version never trails the English one.

Google's AI answers set no extra bar for these pages. Google's [AI features guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) asks only that a page be indexed and able to show a snippet.

For a PI firm in New Orleans, the page that answered <q data-no-check>what to do after a truck accident in New Orleans</q> ranked #1, and Google's AI cited it 3 times. A dated green card or asylum page is the same kind of answer page.

## Immigration work where other rules meet

An immigration firm's plan changes in two places: clients facing a criminal case at the same time, and firms that practice outside the US. Each needs its own pages and its own words.

### Where a criminal record meets immigration status

Federal law lists criminal grounds for deportation in [8 U.S.C. 1227(a)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1227), from aggravated felonies to drug, firearm and domestic violence offenses. A removal defense page can explain which kinds of conviction the statute names, and say when the client's criminal lawyer should be involved.

The criminal half of the same searches is covered on our page about [SEO for criminal defense lawyers](/seo-for-criminal-defense-lawyers/), which plans charge pages for defense firms.

### Immigration firms in the UK, Canada and Australia

In the UK, Canada and Australia, law firms share immigration work with licensed non-lawyers, so a firm's pages must say plainly who regulates it. The table shows who else may advise in each market.

| Market | Who else may advise | What the firm's pages should show |
|---|---|---|
| England and Wales | Advisers licensed by the [Immigration Advice Authority](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/immigration-advice-authority/about), formerly OISC | The SRA number and badge, and published prices |
| Canada | College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants licensees, under [IRPA s. 91](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-2.5/section-91.html) | Each lawyer's provincial law society named |
| Australia | Registered migration agents; [lawyers need no agent registration](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-nsw/professional-support-unit/regulatory-compliance/regulatory-updates-2021) since 22 March 2021 | The practising certificate, not an agent number |

In England and Wales, the [SRA Transparency Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/) (current version from 11 April 2025) make a solicitors' firm publish price and service details for immigration applications and First-tier Tribunal appeals. Asylum work is excluded.

The words change too. Pages for those markets need local terms: "immigration solicitor" and "skilled worker visa" in the UK, "migration lawyer" and "partner visa" in Australia. Check each claim with the SRA, your law society or your regulator before it goes live.

We take on firms outside the US remotely, and Fahim's own record spans 4 countries. In every one of these markets, agencies make the same mistakes.

## What goes wrong when agencies market immigration firms?

Agencies go wrong with immigration firms when they treat language as a setting and trust as an afterthought. Five habits cost an immigration firm qualified leads:

- **A translate widget instead of pages.** Text swapped in place gives Google no separate URL to rank. We build each version as its own page.
- **Two languages on one page.** Google advises one language per page. We split them and link the versions.
- **Approval stories as headlines.** A headline approval implies an outcome no firm can promise the next client. Ours carry context or stay off.
- **A city page for every metro.** Pages with only the city swapped are the doorway pattern Google names. We build where the firm has offices or courts.
- **Pages that never change.** A rule page with no date gives readers no way to know it is current. Ours show when an attorney last checked them.

SEO for immigration lawyers rests on proof a careful client can see: a real attorney, a real page in each language, and a date on every rule.
