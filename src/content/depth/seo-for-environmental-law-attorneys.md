## SEO for environmental law attorneys: check the demand first

Environmental searches are few, and any one of them can be a large matter. Some issues draw steady searches; others draw almost none. So before we plan a page, we check each issue you want more of, in a client's own words and by the statute's name.

"Environmental lawyer" on its own is a poor target. In our keyword research, much of that search comes from people curious about the career. The check ends with a plain decision for each issue: its own page, a section on a wider page, or nothing.

[more "See how the demand check decides"]

We start with your own Search Console data, then a keyword tool filtered to the states you serve, then the results page for each issue. A big number alone doesn't earn a page, and a small one doesn't sink it: when Google ranks pages dedicated to an issue, the issue gets one.

When an EPA or state agency page holds the top spot, our page must answer what the agency leaves out, such as what comes next and whom to call.

[/more]

## Pages built around notices, deadlines and rule changes

For the first 90 days of an account, see [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/). An environmental plan stays small and exact:

[steps]
1. **The issues you take, and the side you act for.**
2. **The rules each page states, read from the statute.**
3. **A page for each notice that starts a search.**
4. **A dated page for each stage of a rule.**
5. **Pages built on agency data, checked twice.**

Each page is written for one reader holding one problem: the company with a notice on its desk, the facility facing a citizen suit, the buyer checking a site before a deal. A page that tries to serve all of them serves none.

[more "See what goes into each environmental page"]

1. You answer one set of questions once: the issues you take, the side you act for, and what may never be said about a client's site. A defense firm and a community group read one notice in opposite ways, so the pages speak for one side.
2. We collect the rules your pages rely on, each from its official text. One example: under [33 U.S.C. 1365(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title33/html/USCODE-2023-title33-chap26-subchapV-sec1365.htm), a Clean Water Act citizen suit can't begin until 60 days after notice to EPA, the state and the alleged violator.
3. A Superfund notice letter or a citizen-suit notice ties a named site to a statute, so each gets a page about the process, never about a client's site. On that page, the form already knows which notice the reader holds and asks when it arrived.
4. A rule moves from proposal to comments, to a final rule and into the Code of Federal Regulations, and sometimes on to court. Each stage gets a dated update and a short change log, and the date readers see matches the date in the page's markup. PFAS rules are the obvious current case.
5. Our injury work with state crash records and federal marine and offshore safety data taught us to check agency data twice. A dashboard can show a year before the download file has it, and two data sets from one agency may count different things. For a plant explosion page on one injury account, we also checked a claim about burn care against the state's own protocol for routing burn patients, and corrected it.

[/more]

[more "See how AI answers choose their sources"]

These answers can also get local facts wrong. Planning a page for El Paso, we found Google's AI claiming local crashes were going down; the state's records showed them going up. A page that gives the agency's own figure, with its source and date, is the correction.

In El Paso, a PI firm's page on a filing deadline leads the sources of Google's AI answer for that question. A page on the 60-day citizen-suit notice has the same shape: one rule, its statute, its date.

## Talks, client alerts and the name search

When a company hears your firm's name from another lawyer, its general counsel looks you up. What they find should show what your lawyers know: a page for each talk, client alert and comment letter, filed under its author's bio.

When a lawyer has spoken at an event, we can turn the talk into a post in that lawyer's own voice. Any figure quoted from memory is checked before the post goes live.

A company client may not be free to post a review, so we ask for a few words on how the firm worked, never on the site or the dispute.

[more "See how bios, the Map and results fit"]

Each staffed office gets its own Business Profile, because name searches and nearby searches run through the Map. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) rule out virtual offices, and we add no town pages where you have no office.

Past results need care. A consent decree may be public, but naming a client's site or settlement still takes the client's written consent, and each result appears with a disclaimer.

[/more]
