## How much search demand does an environmental practice really have?

An environmental practice should measure search demand issue by issue before it builds a page, because some issues draw steady searches and others draw almost none. That check decides where the firm's SEO budget goes. These searches are few, and each one can be a large matter.

The demand check runs in four steps:

[steps]
1. List each issue the firm wants more of, by statute name and in a client's own words.
2. Look up both in a keyword tool and in the firm's Search Console, for each state the firm serves.
3. Search each issue on Google and note who ranks: EPA, a state agency, trade press or other firms.
4. Give each issue its own page, a section on a wider page, or no page at all.

Where an agency page ranks first, the firm's page must answer what the agency leaves out, such as what comes next.

This Example plan shows the output for a defense firm, with no volumes; real numbers come from the firm's own check.

| Issue | Who searches | Page decision |
|---|---|---|
| Superfund liability | Companies named at a cleanup site | One page per notice stage |
| Clean Water Act citizen suits | Facilities that received a notice | One page on the 60-day notice |
| Brownfield purchases | Buyers, lenders and developers | A due diligence page |
| PFAS rules | Water utilities and manufacturers | Dated pages for each rule stage |

Our [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/) page shows how a plan like this becomes pages, links and profile work.

Two of those issues begin with a notice.

## Which notices and deadlines start an environmental client's search?

EPA's Superfund notice letters and the Clean Water Act's 60-day notice of intent to sue are two documents that start an environmental client's search. Each ties a named site or facility to a federal statute, so each deserves its own page.

Each document, who it passes between, and what the firm's page must answer:

| Document | Who sends it, who gets it | What the page must answer |
|---|---|---|
| Superfund general notice letter | EPA, to a party it thinks may be liable | What possible liability means, and what comes next |
| Superfund special notice letter | EPA, when it is ready to negotiate a cleanup | How negotiation works, and who may pay for what |
| Clean Water Act notice of intent to sue | A citizen, to the violator, EPA and the state | What the 60 days allow, for sender and receiver |

EPA's [Superfund enforcement fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/fact-sheet-how-superfund-enforcement-process-works) says a general notice letter tells a party it may be liable. EPA may send a special notice letter when it is ready to negotiate, and those talks are confidential, so the page explains the process, never a client's site.

Under [33 U.S.C. 1365(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/33/1365), a Clean Water Act citizen suit can't start until 60 days after notice to EPA, the state and the alleged violator. Suits over a few listed violations may follow the notice at once.

On notice pages, the form should ask when the document arrived and whether it sets a date. That answer tells the firm how fast to call back.

### Which side should an environmental firm's pages speak for?

An environmental firm's pages should speak for the side it acts for, because the same notice reads differently to the company that receives it and the group that sends it. A site that courts both sides confuses both readers.

Defense firms build pages for notices received, permits, audits and deals. Plaintiff and community firms build pages for contamination, citizen suits and exposure.

Notices arrive one at a time; rules change in stages.

## How should an environmental firm publish each stage of a rule change?

An environmental firm should publish a dated update at each stage of a rule change, from proposal to final rule. Each stage raises new client questions, and a dated page gives trade groups and reporters something current to link to.

EPA's [basics of the regulatory process](https://www.epa.gov/laws-regulations/basics-regulatory-process) lays out the stages, and each one gets its own update:

[steps]
1. **Proposed rule.** EPA lists it in the Federal Register and a Regulations.gov docket. Publish what would change, for whom, and when comments close.
2. **Comment period.** Add the points clients might raise, and link the docket.
3. **Final rule.** EPA publishes it in the Federal Register and the docket. Add what changed and the effective date.
4. **Codified.** Almost all environmental rules land in Title 40 of the Code of Federal Regulations. Add the citation.
5. **Challenged or revisited.** Add each court or agency step as a dated line, and keep the history.

The PFOA and PFOS designation under CERCLA, the Superfund law, shows why dates matter. EPA's [designation page](https://www.epa.gov/superfund/designation-perfluorooctanoic-acid-pfoa-and-perfluorooctanesulfonic-acid-pfos-cercla) records the proposal on 6 September 2022, comments closing on 7 November 2022, and the rule taking effect on 8 July 2024.

On 17 September 2025, EPA [said it would keep the designation](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USEPAAO/bulletins/3f31888) and develop a framework rule for future designations. A page last touched in 2024 tells half the story.

A visible "last updated" line and a short change log belong in the page template, planned when a firm rebuilds through [lawyer web design](/lawyer-web-design-development/).

The firm's lawyers write much of this already, in talks and client alerts.

## Which pages turn an environmental lawyer's talks and alerts into name searches?

A page for each talk, client alert and comment letter, filed under its author's bio, turns an environmental lawyer's work into name searches. A referred company looks up the firm and the attorney by name, and those pages are what it finds.

Give each talk a page with its points, date and event, post each alert as a dated page, and share both on the attorney's LinkedIn.

Branded searches in Search Console, for the firm and each attorney, show whether referrals and talks reach the site. Speaking and the other channels outside Google are covered in [how to get clients as a lawyer](/how-to-get-clients-as-lawyers/).

### Where do advertising rules limit an environmental firm's bios?

Advertising rules reach an environmental firm's bios and alerts on two points in particular: past results and specialist claims. Each US state sets those rules, and the ABA Model Rules are the usual starting point.

The ABA's [comment 3 on Rule 7.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communication_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/comment_on_rule_7_1/) warns that truthful past results can mislead if they lead readers to expect the same outcome for themselves. Name a client's site, settlement or consent decree only with written consent.

Texas, for one, allows certification claims only in the wording its Board of Legal Specialization sets or for a body that Board accredits, under [Rule 7.02(b)](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/advertisements).

You or your ethics counsel approve every page before it goes live. The "expert" in Lawyer SEO Expert names our search work, not a legal certification.

This is general information about marketing, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and country, so check yours with your bar, law society or ethics counsel.

## What will Google's AI pick up from an environmental site, and how soon?

Google's AI picks up an environmental page only after it is indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and Google says no special markup is needed. How soon that happens depends on months of groundwork.

Google sets out those conditions in [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), and adds that no AI text files are needed either. Pages that state one rule and its date in their opening lines give the AI a clean passage to lift.

A deadline page did this for a PI firm in El Paso. For the search on the Texas car accident filing deadline, Google's AI answer cited that firm's page ahead of every other source. An environmental page on the 60-day citizen-suit notice is built the same way.

Timing follows groundwork. In New Orleans, a PI firm had about three months of base work done before its new pages ranked within hours. No one can promise an environmental firm a date.

We check Google's AI answers for each target issue every month. Reach beyond one city changes the plan again.

## Environmental practices beyond one city or one country

Two kinds of environmental practice need the plan adjusted: firms that serve a whole state or country from one office, and firms based outside the US.

### A statewide or national practice and the Map

A statewide or national environmental practice still needs a Business Profile for each staffed office, because its name search and nearby searches run through the Map.

Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) exclude virtual offices and keep a service area within about two hours' drive for most businesses. Choose the categories Google offers that sit closest to the firm's work, and list each issue as a service.

Reviews are harder here, since a company client may not be free to post one. Ask for a few words on how the firm worked, never on the site or dispute.

Beyond its offices, the firm reaches clients through issue and notice pages. [SEO for intellectual property lawyers](/seo-for-intellectual-property-lawyers/) sets out how a niche B2B firm picks national or local pages, a test that fits environmental work.

### Environmental solicitors and lawyers abroad

Environmental firms in the UK, Canada and Australia can run the same plan, with their own regulators and rule stages in place of EPA's. In England and Wales, the SRA's [Code of Conduct](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/) says a solicitor's publicity must be accurate and not misleading (paragraph 8.8). Elsewhere, check the local regulator's code.

We work with firms abroad remotely, and Fahim's own work has spanned 4 countries, Ireland and New Zealand among them.

Whatever the reach, the same few mistakes sink the work early.

## What sinks environmental law SEO before it starts?

Environmental law SEO sinks early when a firm chases the wrong term, builds pages before checking demand, or leaves rule pages undated. Each mistake wastes budget in a field where every search counts.

The habits to avoid:

- Targeting "environmental lawyer" alone, a term that in our keyword research draws people curious about the career.
- A page for every statute, written before the demand check.
- Rule pages with no dates, so readers can't tell if they are current.
- Pages for both sides of the same dispute on one site.

SEO for environmental law attorneys works best small and exact: few pages, each tied to a real search, a real document or a real rule stage.
