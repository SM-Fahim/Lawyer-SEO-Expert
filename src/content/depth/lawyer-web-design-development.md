## How do you move to a new law firm website without losing rankings?

A law firm keeps its rankings through a redesign by sending every old URL that ranks, gets visits or earns links to its closest new page with a permanent redirect. [Google's guide to site moves with URL changes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) sets out the method.

The plan below assumes the domain stays the same.

[steps]
1. **List every address the old site has.** Google points to the sitemap, the server logs and the content system as sources. Add attorney bios, results pages and old posts, which a menu can leave out.
2. **Mark the pages that earn something.** Flag every URL with search clicks, visits, links from other sites or tracked calls. Those pages hold the firm's rankings, so each one needs a close match on the new site.
3. **Write the redirect map.** Pair each old URL with its closest new page, in a sheet the firm keeps. Google warns against pointing old URLs in bulk at one unrelated page, such as the home page, and may treat that as a soft 404.
4. **Keep what made each page rank.** Carry its topic, its main heading and its key facts onto the new page, even when the design changes completely.
5. **Build on a staging copy that Google can't index.** Lift the block at launch. Google reminds site owners to remove noindex tags and robots.txt rules that were only needed for the move.
6. **Test every redirect before launch.** Each one should be a server-side permanent redirect, a 301 or 308, straight to the final page. Google says to keep any chain ideally to 3 hops or fewer, and under 5.
7. **Launch with every link pointing at the new addresses.** Update internal links, the sitemap and each page's self-referencing canonical tag. Then change the website link on the firm's Business Profile and on its directory and bar listings.
8. **Watch Search Console through the first weeks.** Google says most pages of a small to medium site can take a few weeks to move. Fix any page that drops out or returns an error.
9. **Leave the redirects in place for at least a year.** That is Google's general advice, and Google says 301 and other permanent redirects don't lose PageRank.

> **A new domain adds one step.** Google's Change of Address tool is needed only when a site moves from one domain or subdomain to another. A switch to HTTPS, or between www and non-www, doesn't need it.

The length of the redirect map is one reason two builds of the same size can carry different prices.

## What the firm should own when the site is handed over

A law firm should own every part of its website at handover, from the domain and hosting to the code, the words and the tracking accounts. With us, the firm owns everything we build.

Check each item against the quote and the contract before work starts.

| Item | Whose name it should be in | What goes wrong if it isn't |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | The firm's, at a registrar it logs into | The firm can't renew or move its own address |

## Which disclosures belong on a law firm website, and where?

The disclosures a law firm website needs depend on where its lawyers practice, because each US state sets its own advertising rules. Most states model theirs on the ABA Model Rules, which point to a few items every US firm site should check.

Here is each item, with the part of the site it touches.

[checklist]
- **Footer: a responsible lawyer or firm.** [ABA Model Rule 7.2(d)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_2_advertising/) asks any communication under the rule to name at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for its content, with contact information.
- **Attorney bios: specialist wording.** Under Rule 7.2(c), a bio may call a lawyer a certified specialist only when a state-approved or ABA-accredited body did the certifying. The bio has to name that body.
- **Results pages: wording your counsel has approved.** States differ on how past results may be shown, so the results page is the first one to send to counsel.
- **Home page: filing in Texas.** Texas firms file ads with the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee within 10 days of dissemination, under [Rule 7.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/filing-requirements-for-advertisements-and-solicitation-communications). Rule 7.05 exempts the rest of a firm's website, but not the home page, unless its content is exempt for another reason.
- **Old labels in New York.** New York's rule changes have applied since June 1, 2026. A speaker quoted in a [September 2026 NYSBA article](https://nysba.org/understanding-changes-to-new-york-state-lawyer-advertising-rules/) says "attorney advertising" no longer has to go on everything a firm sends. A redesign is the time to ask counsel which old labels stay.

A new Texas home page can go through advance review too. Rule 7.04 lets a lawyer submit it at least 30 days before first use.

### Which items must a solicitor's website show in England and Wales?

A solicitor's firm in England and Wales must show its SRA number and the SRA's digital badge in a prominent place on its website. That is rule 4.1 of the [SRA Transparency Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/), in a version in force since 11 April 2025.

Three more items follow from the rules and the SRA's own guidance.

- **Complaints.** Rule 2 requires the firm's complaints procedure, including how and when a client can complain to the Legal Ombudsman and to the SRA.
- **Prices for listed services.** Rule 1 covers services such as residential conveyancing, uncontested probate and some immigration work. Under rule 1.6, that cost information must be clear and accessible, and in a prominent place on the website.
- **A badge that works on the new address.** The SRA's [digital badge](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/resources/transparency/digital-badge/) became mandatory on 25 November 2019, and it works only on websites linked to the firm's mySRA account. Before launching on a new domain, update the address in mySRA and allow at least 24 hours.

In Canada and Australia, law societies set the advertising rules for firm websites, and the rules differ by province and state. Check the local code before launch.

This is general information about marketing, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and country, so check yours with your bar, law society or ethics counsel.

## What does the ADA ask of a law firm website?

In the US, the ADA counts a lawyer's office as a public accommodation, and the Justice Department says the law reaches what such businesses offer online. The department sets no detailed technical standard for a law firm website.

The office of a lawyer is listed in [42 U.S.C. 12181(7)(F)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/12181), the ADA's definition of public accommodations.

The [Justice Department's web guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/), dated March 18, 2022, says the ADA's requirements apply to goods and services offered on the web. The guidance names WCAG and the federal Section 508 standards as helpful guidance, not as a regulation.

WCAG is the W3C's accessibility standard, and version 2.2 is its current Recommendation. Content that meets WCAG 2.2 meets 2.1 and 2.0 as well, [the W3C says](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

On a law firm website, each barrier the guidance names sits in a predictable place.

- **Poor color contrast.** Pale text over a hero photo, where the phone number may sit.
- **Images with no text alternative.** Attorney photos, award badges and office maps that a screen reader can't describe.
- **Videos with no captions.** Attorney introductions and explainer videos for clients.
- **Form fields a screen reader can't name.** The intake form, where a lost visitor means a lost lead.
- **Parts of the page that need a mouse.** Menus, chat boxes and booking tools a keyboard can't reach.

Ask any builder which WCAG version and level a site is tested against, and to show the results. Your counsel can say what the ADA means for your firm.

## Speed, rebuild timing and life after launch

Three questions follow the main decisions on a law firm website: how fast it must be, whether to rebuild, and what happens after launch. One warning comes last.

### How fast should a law firm site load for Google?

A law firm site is fast enough for Google when its pages meet three Core Web Vitals targets. Main content loads within 2.5 seconds, taps get a response within 200 milliseconds, and layout shift stays at 0.1 or less.

[Google's web.dev guide](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) says to measure each one at the 75th percentile of real page loads, with mobile and desktop counted separately.

Search Console's Core Web Vitals report uses that real-visitor data. [Its help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9205520) says URLs without enough data are left out, so a small firm site may see few of its pages in the report.

Speed counts, but relevance comes first. Google's [page experience guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience) says its ranking systems use Core Web Vitals, and that Search still shows the most relevant page even when its experience is weak.

For a firm, the page to test first is the practice page a client reaches from a phone.

### Rebuild or repair: when is a new law firm site worth it?

A new law firm site is worth it when the current one can't hold the pages the firm needs, can't leave its builder, or fails on phones. Otherwise, fixing the site in place keeps its rankings with less risk.

These signs point to a rebuild.

- **No room for case-type pages.** The site allows one page per practice area and nothing below it.
- **A platform that belongs to the builder.** Changing builders means leaving the site behind.
- **Templates that fail on phones.** Slow or broken layouts that a few edits won't fix.

A repair fits when the site already ranks and brings calls, and the problems sit in its words, its speed or a few templates. A repair keeps every address the same, so no redirect map is needed.

### What happens to a law firm site after launch?

After launch, a law firm site needs software updates, new pages and regular checks that its forms and numbers still work. A firm can run that itself, or keep the site on monthly SEO with us.

Monthly SEO covers design and content improvements, maintenance, new practice and location pages, and call and form tracking. See [what the monthly SEO work covers](/law-firm-seo-services/).

### Where law firm rebuilds lose calls

A law firm rebuild can lose calls in six ways, and every one of them starts before launch day, while there is still time to fix it.

- **No redirect map.** Old pages return errors on launch day, and the rankings they held start to slip.
- **A staging block left on.** A noindex tag or robots.txt rule from the build keeps the new site out of Google.
- **Headings rewritten for looks.** Pages lose the searches and facts that ranked them.
- **Forms nobody tested.** Leads go to an old inbox, or nowhere.
- **Tracking numbers dropped.** Calls still come in, but nobody can tell which page brought them.
- **Logins left with the builder.** The firm needs the old builder's help to make the next change.

Each one is avoidable when lawyer web design starts from what the old site already ranks for.
