## How does law firm PPC decide which ad shows and what a click costs?

Law firm PPC is paid search advertising, mostly on Google Ads, where the firm pays each time someone clicks its ad. Google runs an auction on every search, and a score called Ad Rank decides whether an ad shows and where.

[Google's Ad Rank page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1752122) lists the bid, the quality of the ad and its landing page, the competition and the context of the search among its inputs. Google says relevant keywords and ads can win a higher spot at a lower price.

<dfn>Quality Score</dfn> is the 1 to 10 rating Google shows for each keyword. [Google calls it](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) a diagnostic tool, built from expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, and says it is not an input in the auction.

Legal clicks cost more than most. [WordStream's 2025 benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2025-google-ads-benchmarks) drew on 16,446 US search campaigns from April 2024 to March 2025. Attorneys and legal services had **the highest median cost per click of any industry**, and the highest cost per lead.

Ads sit apart from the organic results, which Google says it never takes money to include or rank. Ranking there is the job of our [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/).

With clicks this costly, settle ownership first.

## Who should own a law firm's Google Ads account, billing and data?

The law firm should own its Google Ads account, its payments profile and every conversion and tracking setting in it. That is how we work: your firm's own account, Google billing your firm directly, and our fee kept separate.

Google Ads has five access levels. [Google's access guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9978556) gives only Admin users the power to grant access, change access levels and complete advertiser verification. Standard users can edit campaigns, but can grant nothing beyond email-only access.

Run through this list on any account, whoever manages it.

[checklist]
- **The firm holds Admin access.** A partner or office manager, not only the agency, can add and remove users.
- **The payments profile is in the firm's name.** Google bills the firm's own card or account for every click.
- **The agency has its own login.** Standard access is enough to run campaigns, and the login goes when the work ends.
- **Advertiser verification is done in the firm's name.** Google shows the advertiser's name and location in its ad disclosures.
- **Conversion actions live in the firm's account.** Calls, forms and imported outcomes stay with the firm's history.
- **Analytics and tag manager belong to the firm.** The data feeding the account stays if the agency changes.
- **Call tracking numbers sit in a firm-owned account.** The numbers in the ads keep ringing after a switch.
- **Someone at the firm reviews the user list.** Former staff and agencies lose access the day they leave.

With control settled, next comes what the account should never pay for.

## Which searches should a law firm's ads never pay for?

A law firm's ads should never pay for searches from job seekers, law students or people looking for free forms. <dfn>Negative keywords</dfn> are the words that keep an ad off those searches, and every law firm account needs a starting list.

Here is a starter list by theme, to extend from the firm's own search terms.

| Theme | Words to start with | Why block them |
|---|---|---|
| Jobs and pay | jobs, salary, hiring, careers, internship | Job seekers, not people with a legal problem |
| Becoming a lawyer | school, degree, course, bar exam | Students researching a career |
| Doing it alone | template, form, sample, DIY | People planning to act without a lawyer |
| Free legal help | legal aid, pro bono | Only where the firm doesn't take that work |
| Looking things up | court records, case lookup, definition | Research, not a search for a lawyer |
| Other practice areas | the areas the firm doesn't handle | Clicks the firm can't turn into clients |
| Places outside the area | cities and states the firm doesn't serve | Searchers the firm can't take on |

> **Don't block "free" on its own.** A search for a free consultation can come from someone ready to call a firm that offers one.

Negative keywords work more strictly than normal keywords. [Google's negative keyword guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) says they don't match close variants, so plurals and synonyms have to be added by hand.

Blocking waste pays off only when the account can tell a good lead from a wasted one.

## How do you count qualified leads from Google Ads instead of clicks?

A law firm counts qualified leads from Google Ads by tying each call and form to its ad click and marking which leads fit. That result goes back into Google Ads, so spend can follow the searches that work.

Qualified means the matter is one the firm handles, in an area it covers. These are the steps, in order.

[steps]
1. **Count calls from the ads and from the site.** [Google Ads can count a call](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) to the number on the firm's site after an ad click, once it lasts longer than a minimum length the firm sets.
2. **Count every form, with the page it came from.** A form with no source can't show which search worked.
3. **Save Google's click ID with each lead.** Google gives every ad click a unique ID, the GCLID, which is stored with the lead's details.
4. **Mark which leads were qualified.** The intake team or the case software records it, against a definition the firm sets once.
5. **Send the results back to Google Ads.** [Offline conversion import](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2998031) reports which leads went on to become clients. Google calls enhanced conversions for leads its upgraded version, which uses details such as email addresses.
6. **Move the budget.** Searches that bring good leads get more of it, and the rest get less, or get blocked.

Ask counsel before sending any prospective client's details to an ad platform.

Each ad should land on the page that answers its search. Our [lawyer web design](/lawyer-web-design-development/) work builds those pages to load fast and take the call.

## Which Google Ads policies catch law firm ads?

As of November 2026, [Google's ad policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6008942) contain no policy named for legal services, so law firm ads fall under the general rules. Six of those rules touch legal work directly.

| Policy | What Google says | Who it catches |
|---|---|---|
| [Legal requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6023676) | Ads must follow local laws in every area they target | Every firm; serious breaches can mean suspension |
| [Misrepresentation](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955) | No improbable results, no hidden payment terms | Result claims and fee wording in ad copy |
| [Bail bond services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527531) | No ads for bail bond surety, immigration bail included | Ads offering to act as surety for bail |
| [Government documents](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13156083) | Advertisers need Google certification first | Ads offering ESTA, passport or visa lottery filings |
| [Loan modification](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/2464998) | Upfront fees disapproved unless a law firm provides it | Foreclosure and mortgage practices |
| [Debt services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9520029) | US ads only from approved nonprofit credit counselors | Ads worded as debt settlement or management |

The debt services rule changes by country. Google lists licensed lawyers as eligible in Germany, Japan and South Korea, each with its own checks.

A competitor's firm name is a separate case. [Google's trademark policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118) doesn't restrict trademarks used as keywords, but may restrict one in ad text after a complaint. Whether your bar allows the tactic is a question for counsel.

### Who at the firm completes Google's advertiser verification?

An Admin on the firm's Google Ads account completes advertiser verification, which [Google is rolling out](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9703665) to every advertiser in stages. Google may pause an account until it is done.

- **Why it has to be an Admin.** [Google's task list](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15577076) says only a Google Ads account admin can complete the tasks.
- **Who signs.** An admin of the account or of the paying payments profile. [Their photo ID](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280) can come from any country.
- **What an agency must answer.** An agency has to select Yes for "Is your organization an agency?", or the account may be paused or suspended.
- **When it comes back.** Changing the legal name on a billing profile starts a new verification at once, which matters after a merger or rename.

In two markets, Local Services Ads run beside search ads.

## Local Services Ads for lawyers: where they run and what Google checks

Local Services Ads are a separate Google ad format, open to lawyers in some markets, that charges per lead rather than per click. As of November 2026, Google lists legal categories in the US and Greater London only.

[Google's category lists](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) read like this by country.

| Country | Legal categories Google lists | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| United States | 17, with no area limit noted | Personal injury, DUI, family, immigration, bankruptcy |
| United Kingdom | 15 lawyer categories plus personal injury, Greater London only | Employment, probate, property, road traffic |
| Canada | None | Home service categories only |
| Ireland | None | Home service categories only |

In the US, Google screens a law firm before its ads run. [Its screening list](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778) covers identity checks on the business owner and a state bar license check for each lawyer in the firm. It adds professional liability insurance where local law requires it.

[Google's badge page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9376654) says it is moving every advertiser to one Google Verified badge. Its Money Back Guarantee now covers only services booked before December 7, 2025.

Both formats carry the firm's name, so both answer to the bar's advertising rules.

## What do bar rules mean for a law firm's ad copy?

A law firm's search ad is a communication about its lawyers' services, so the advertising rules that bind those lawyers apply to every headline. In the US, each state sets its own, and most base them on the ABA Model Rules.

[ABA Model Rule 7.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communication_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/) bars false or misleading communications. A true statement can still mislead when it leaves out a fact needed to keep the whole message accurate.

Short ads make that harder. [Google's responsive search ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791) take headlines of up to 30 characters, and Google mixes headlines and descriptions into its own combinations. A results headline can show without the description that gave it context.

These are the checks we run on ad copy for US firms.

- **Results claims.** Keep them out of headlines unless counsel approves the exact wording, since any headline can run alone.
- **Specialist words.** "Specialist" and "certified" carry the same limits in an ad as on the firm's site.
- **Filing in Texas.** Under [Rule 7.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/filing-requirements-for-advertisements-and-solicitation-communications), a Texas lawyer's ads go to the state bar for filing no later than 10 days after they first run, unless Rule 7.05 exempts them. Ask counsel whether search ads qualify.
- **Your approval.** You or your ethics counsel approve every ad before it runs.

### How do the rules change for ads outside the US?

Outside the US, the regulator changes and so do the limits on paid ads. In England and Wales, an [SRA warning notice](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/marketing-public/) from 19 December 2024 says marketing that highlights "no win, no fee" should clearly explain it.

The same notice applies the ban on unsolicited approaches to clients a third party refers. That matters for firms that buy leads instead of running their own ads.

In Queensland, [section 66 of the Personal Injuries Proceedings Act 2002](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2026-10-29/act-2002-024) limits personal injury ads to the practitioner's name, contact details and area of practice. Section 65 lists the allowed methods. The Act gives "no win, no fee" advertising as an example of a breach.

In Canada, law societies set the rules for each province and territory, so check the local code.

This is general information about marketing, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and country, so check yours with your bar, law society or ethics counsel.

## Fees, takeovers and how ads sit beside SEO

Three practical questions come after the rules: how a law firm PPC fee is set, what a takeover involves and how ads share the work with SEO.

### Flat fee or a share of ad spend: which suits a law firm?

A flat management fee stays the same as a law firm's ad budget grows, while a share-of-spend fee rises with the budget, whether or not lead quality improves.

[Forward Lawyer Marketing](https://fwd-lawyermarketing.com/pricing/), for one, publishes a sliding scale from 25% of monthly budget down to 12.5% as spend grows (checked November 2026).

Our management fee starts from $500 a month, in US dollars, and Fahim gives it to you in writing before work starts. With any agency, get the fee in writing.

### What happens when we take over an existing account?

When we take over a law firm's Google Ads account, the history stays in place and the first job is a review, not changes. That review covers four areas.

- **Access and billing.** Who holds Admin, whose payments profile pays, and which old users still have access.
- **Conversions.** Which actions count, and whether any call or form is counted twice.
- **Search terms and locations.** Which searches cost money without a lead, and whether ads show outside the firm's area.
- **Policy status.** Disapproved ads, warnings, and where advertiser verification stands.

### How do Google Ads and SEO split the work for a law firm?

Google Ads can put a law firm in front of searchers as soon as its ads are approved, and stops when the budget does. SEO takes longer to earn rankings, and those rankings don't charge by the click.

Ads don't lift organic rankings. [Google says](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) advertising with it has no effect on a site's presence in its search results.

They do share data. An ad account's best searches show which practice pages to build for organic search first. The monthly cost of that work is on our [law firm SEO pricing](/seo-pricing/) page.

### Where law firm ad budgets leak

A law firm's ad budget can leak in five ways, and each one shows in the account's own data for anyone with access to look.

- **Broad keywords with no negatives.** Clicks from job seekers and students.
- **Every call counted the same.** A ten-second wrong number looks like a new client.
- **Location settings drawn too wide.** Ads show to people the firm can't serve.
- **An account in the agency's name.** The history and settings leave when the agency does.
- **Ad copy nobody at the firm approved.** The bar rules bind the lawyers, not the agency.

That visibility is why law firm PPC belongs in an account the firm owns.
