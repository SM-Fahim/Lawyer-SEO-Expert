## How do planning clients and probate clients search differently?

Planning clients search slowly after a life event, while probate clients search fast after a death, so estate planning SEO builds two sets of pages. Each set brings a different kind of call.

The table compares the two, using real searches from our keyword research.

| What differs | Planning pages | Probate pages |
|---|---|---|
| What starts the search | A life event: a child, a marriage, a diagnosis | A death in the family |
| Who is searching | The person making the plan | An executor or an adult child |
| Pace | No outside deadline | Court steps with their own timelines |
| A real search | <q data-no-check>cost of making a will with a lawyer</q> | <q data-no-check>how much does a probate lawyer cost</q> |
| Where the law sits | State law on wills and trusts | The county probate court |
| What the page leads with | What each document does, and its price | The executor's first steps, and the fee basis |
| The next step offered | A planning consultation | A call the same week |

Cost searches sit closest to a call on both sides. A planning search such as <q data-no-check>how to make a living trust without a lawyer</q> comes earlier, from someone testing the do-it-yourself route. A page that says plainly what a homemade trust can miss gives that reader a reason to come back.

Trust administration and elder law are lanes of their own. Successor trustees and adult children of aging parents ask different questions, so each lane gets a page only if the firm takes that work.

What we do each month to build and rank both page sets is listed under [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/). Both sets of readers reach the fee page sooner or later.

## What should an estate planning firm's fee page show?

An estate planning firm's fee page should show the price or range for each service, what it includes, the stages and who does the work. In England and Wales, the SRA requires those details for uncontested probate.

Cost searches appear in both columns above, so a fee page answers a question readers already have. That puts a fee page close to the call.

Under the [SRA Transparency Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/), firms must publish price and service details for uncontested estate administration where all the assets are in the UK. Rule 1.6 says the information must be clear, accessible and in a prominent place on the website.

This checklist follows the SRA's list in rule 1.5. In England and Wales it is required for that probate work. For a US planning firm, it is a sound structure for any fee page:

[checklist]
- The total cost, or an average or range where a total isn't practical.
- The basis for charges: fixed fees or hourly rates.
- The experience and qualifications of the people doing the work, and of their supervisors.
- Likely disbursements and their cost, such as court filing fees.
- Whether VAT applies, and how much (England and Wales).
- What the price includes, the key stages and likely timescales.
- Anything a client might expect that the price leaves out.

In the US, each state sets its own rules on fee claims, and comparisons are one angle to check. The comments to Texas [Rule 7.01](https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/communications-concerning-a-lawyers-services/), for one, warn that an unsubstantiated comparison of a lawyer's fees with other lawyers' fees may mislead. A clear fee page helps referral partners too.

## How do referrals, seminars and search work together?

Referrals and search work together for an estate planning firm: a referred client can check the firm's name on Google before calling, and seminar pages turn searchers into attendees. Both paths end on the firm's own pages.

That name search should show a complete Business Profile, a page for each attorney, and directory listings with the current address and phone number.

Referral deals have rules. In Texas, [Rule 7.03(e)](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/solicitation-and-other-prohibited-communications) bars paying nonlawyers for referring clients, while allowing reasonable fees for advertising. It permits reciprocal referral agreements with other lawyers or nonlawyer professionals only if they aren't exclusive, clients are told about them, and the lawyer's judgment on referrals stays independent.

For a planning firm, that covers the financial advisers and accountants who send clients. Other states write their own versions, so check yours before any arrangement goes on paper.

Seminar and workshop pages work as event pages: the date, the place or link, the topics, the presenter and a sign-up form. After each event, a short summary page can keep the topic searchable once the date has passed.

Referrals, talks and the other channels beyond search are covered in our guide to [how lawyers get clients](/how-to-get-clients-as-lawyers/). Seminars are local events, and probate is local too, county by county.

## Does every county need its own probate page?

An estate planning firm needs a probate page only for each county where it handles probate and can describe that court's process. A page that swaps the county name and nothing else is a doorway page under Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

A county probate page earns its place with local detail: where the court sits, its forms and local rules, the usual steps and how the firm supports an executor there. It competes with other local firms and with the court's own website, so it has to add what the court can't.

On the Map, the office's Business Profile does the local work. Google's guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names relevance, distance and prominence, and says more reviews and positive ratings can help.

Each attorney whom clients can reach at the office during its hours can hold a practitioner profile. Google's [profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't allow extra profiles per specialty, so wills and probate share one.

For a PI firm in New Orleans, Google's own count shows 139 calls from its Business Profile from June to October 2026, a 39% rise year on year. A planning firm's profile is run the same way.

Keeping county pages apart from planning pages in the menu is part of our [lawyer web design](/lawyer-web-design-development/) work.

Reviews feed that profile, and in estate work they need extra care.

## Reviews and sensitive topics in a planning practice

A planning practice should ask for reviews when a plan is signed, not while a family is grieving. Pages on illness, capacity and family conflict need the same care, because they decide whether a worried family calls.

Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) forbids rewarding reviews, inviting only satisfied clients and telling reviewers what to write. Offer the review link on paper as well as by email.

The FTC's [consumer reviews rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), in force since 21 October 2024, bars fake reviews and testimonials, and bars paying for reviews that must be positive.

## What did 2026 change for estate planning pages and AI answers?

The IRS puts the 2026 federal basic exclusion amount at $15,000,000, set by Public Law 119-21 in July 2025. An estate planning page that quotes any other 2026 figure is wrong, and stale figures cost the firm the reader's trust.

The IRS sets out the change on its [estate and gift tax page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/whats-new-estate-and-gift-tax), as of November 2026. Lead planning pages with the reasons that apply to every family, such as incapacity, guardianship for children and avoiding probate, and keep estate tax for the clients it affects.

Google's guide to [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says no special markup is needed, though the features only draw on pages that are indexed and can show a snippet. Questions that name a state or a county, such as whether a trust suits that state or how long probate takes in that county, suit a local firm's page best.

## Edge cases for wills, trusts and probate firms

Wills and probate work has three edge cases worth planning for. Clients come back after a divorce, firms abroad use different terms, and every firm has to judge its first months.

### When a divorce reopens the plan

A divorce can send a client back to the estate plan, because the will, trust and beneficiary forms were written for a marriage that has ended. A planning firm should give that update its own page, written for someone whose divorce is final.

The page explains which documents to review and in what order, then invites a review meeting. How family firms rank for the divorce itself, and where the two practices meet, is covered in [SEO for family lawyers](/seo-for-family-lawyers/).

### Wills and probate pages for England and Wales, Australia and Canada

Outside the US, this practice goes by other names: "wills and probate" in England and Wales, and "wills and estates" in Australia and Canada. Page titles should use the local term, and "solicitor" where it applies.

In England and Wales, the government's online service helps people make a lasting power of attorney themselves. Per [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney/register), the form is printed, signed and sent for registration, which takes 8 to 10 weeks if there are no mistakes. A solicitor's LPA page should say what a solicitor adds to that process.

Canadian estate pages should follow the province's own probate terms and its law society's code. Estate firms abroad can hire us, since the work is remote. Fahim's growth work spans four countries, from Ireland (Breakthrough Maths) to New Zealand (Idiya).

### How the first months unfold for a planning practice

A planning practice spends its first months on groundwork: both page sets, the fee page, the profile and call tracking. Pages built on that groundwork can rise quickly.

The New Orleans PI firm put in about three months of groundwork first. Then the question page in the hero went to #1 on Google within 16 hours of going live. Nobody can promise that timing for a planning firm, and a promised date only weakens trust over time.

## What weakens an estate planning site over time?

An estate planning site weakens when its figures go stale, its probate pages copy each other, its reviews follow a script and its pages push readers too early. Each costs trust with families and referral partners.

Some agency advice makes this worse. One agency guide in this search suggests an online "estate plan builder" with no word of caution about lawyer review. The same guide suggests set wording for client reviews, which Google's policy forbids.

Watch, too, for statistics with no primary source, and for template sections copied from another practice's page.

Good SEO for estate planning lawyers stays current, local and calm, and it brings the call when a family is ready.
