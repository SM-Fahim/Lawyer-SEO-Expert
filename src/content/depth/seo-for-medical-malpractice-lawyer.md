## What should a malpractice firm's pages and forms screen for?

A malpractice firm's pages and forms should screen for the facts its attorneys need before a consult: when the harm happened, who provided the care, what harm followed and where. Medical malpractice SEO works when that screening starts on the page, not on the phone.

Search brings both kinds of reader. Searches like <q data-no-check>what type of lawyer handles medical malpractice</q> and <q data-no-check>how much does a medical malpractice lawyer cost</q> come from people weighing a claim. Searches like <q data-no-check>can you sue a lawyer for malpractice</q> or <q data-no-check>how to become a medical malpractice lawyer</q> bring visits and no qualified leads, so we leave them alone.

The checklist is the screen we build into each form, with the page around it explaining why each question matters:

[checklist]
- **The date of the harm, and the date it was noticed.** Ask for both; your attorneys decide which one your state's deadline uses.
- **The state where the care happened.** Pre-suit steps and fee limits change at the state line.
- **The provider and the facility.** A hospital, a clinic, a nursing home or a doctor's office.
- **What harm followed,** as a few plain choices plus a short text box.
- **Whether medical records have been requested,** and when.
- **Whether another lawyer has looked at the case,** and what they said.
- **When and how to call back.**

The page should say what the firm takes, in plain words, so a wrong-fit reader learns that before the form. We track calls and forms by page, so the firm sees which pages bring qualified leads and which bring noise.

The intake side, from first reply to case review, is covered in our guide to [CRM software for injury firms](/crm-software-for-personal-injury-lawyers/). Two of the checklist items, the state and the records, matter most where a state adds steps before suit.

## Which state <span class="mm-nowrap">pre-suit</span> rules change a malpractice intake form?

Some states add steps to a malpractice claim, before or soon after filing, that other injury claims don't face. Those steps decide what a malpractice firm's intake form and pages should ask, starting with New York's certificate of merit.

The table shows three states' rules, checked on 8 November 2026. Your state may have none of these, or different ones.

| State | The rule | What the page and form should reflect |
|---|---|---|
| New York | [CPLR 3012-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVP/3012-A): certificate of merit after consulting a licensed physician | Ask about records early; the firm needs a medical review |
| Texas | [CPRC 74.351](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.74.htm#74.351): expert report within 120 days of each defendant's answer | Screen for claims an expert can support before taking them |
| Florida | [Stat. 766.106](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/766.106): notice of intent, then no suit for 90 days | Explain the presuit wait so callers expect it |

In Texas, if no expert report is served in time, the court must, on the defendant's motion, dismiss the claim with prejudice and award the defendant's fees and costs. That makes early screening worth the effort, and the pages are where it starts.

In New York, the certificate rule has an exception: if a limitation deadline would bar the claim before a consult, the certificate can follow within 90 days of service. A New York page can explain why speed still matters without giving advice on any one case.

A malpractice firm should have one page per state it takes cases in, each citing the rule and dated. Our [law firm SEO services](/law-firm-seo-services/) page shows how pages like these are planned and checked each month. Fee rules differ by state too, and they reach further into the site.

## Why can't a malpractice site reuse an injury firm's fee wording?

A malpractice site can't reuse injury fee wording, because New York, California and Florida cap malpractice contingency fees in ways a flat "one-third" line can break. The caps differ by state, so the fee page follows the rule where the firm practices.

The table compares three states' rules, as of November 2026.

| State | The rule | The limit | What it changes on the fee page |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | [Judiciary Law 474-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/474-A) | A sliding scale, 30% falling to 10% | No flat one-third; show the scale |
| California | [Bus. & Prof. Code 6146](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=6146) | 25% if settled before a complaint or arbitration demand; 33% after | Name both stages, not one figure |
| Florida | [Constitution, Art. I, s. 26](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution) | Client keeps at least 70% of a first tier, 90% above | Say what the client keeps, under the state's rule |

New York and California each allow a higher fee in limited cases: a court's finding of extraordinary circumstances in New York, or a motion after trial or arbitration in California.

So a fee line copied from an injury page can be wrong on a malpractice page in all three states. The same goes for calculators and settlement examples built for injury cases: each one needs the state's malpractice rule behind it.

On a firm that does both kinds of work, the fee page for each practice should say which rules apply to it. Your attorneys approve the wording, and this page's examples are not a template.

## How should a malpractice site divide its pages by harm?

A malpractice site should give each kind of harm it takes its own page, named the way families describe it, such as a missed diagnosis. A page for harm the firm turns away only brings calls it can't take.

These are the harm pages we plan from, each with the question it must answer first:

- **Missed or delayed diagnosis.** What a delay can change, and what records show it.
- **Birth injury.** Written with care for parents, with your state's deadline rule cited.
- **Surgical error.** How a known risk differs from a mistake.
- **Medication error.** Wrong drug, wrong dose, and who in the chain made the call.
- **Emergency room care.** What a rushed discharge or missed sign looks like on paper.

Each harm page cites its medical sources and names the attorney who checked it. A city version adds nothing unless the firm has a local fact to give, such as the courts where it files.

When one firm runs injury and malpractice work on one site, the two sides must not compete for the same searches.

## What do referring lawyers check before they send a malpractice case?

Referring lawyers who search a malpractice firm's name see what anyone sees: the Business Profile, attorney bios, verdict pages and reviews. The client may run the same search again before calling.

Verdict pages need the most care. Under ABA Model Rule 7.1, [comment 3](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communication_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/comment_on_rule_7_1/), a truthful verdict report may still mislead when it creates an unjustified expectation. A disclaimer or qualifying language can prevent that. Each state adopts its own version, so each verdict page carries the context your rules require.

Referral fees have rules of their own. ABA [Model Rule 1.5(e)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_5_fees/comment_on_rule_1_5/) lets lawyers in different firms split a fee only by work done or with joint responsibility. The client must agree to each lawyer's share, confirmed in writing, and the total must be reasonable. States vary, so a page about working with referring lawyers stays general.

Reviews round out the name search. Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) bars asking only happy clients, so a malpractice firm asks every client the same way. Replies never mention a patient, a diagnosis or a case.

The "expert" in our own name refers to search work, never to a legal credential. A name search turns up the firm's answers to common questions too, and deadline pages are where Google's AI comes in.

## Which malpractice deadline pages does Google's AI cite?

The malpractice deadline pages worth building answer one question for one state, cite the statute and show a date. One such page, for a PI firm in El Paso, is the first source Google's AI answer cites. Its search was <q data-no-check>Texas El Paso car accident filing deadline</q>.

A malpractice deadline page for your state is the same kind of page. The search pattern is "medical malpractice statute of limitations [state]", and the page that wins it names the statute, the state, any pre-suit step and the date it was checked.

Google's [AI features guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says, as of November 2026, that a page needs only to be indexed and eligible for a snippet, and that AI Overviews often don't trigger. So some deadline searches show no AI answer at all, and the page still has to win the regular results.

## Malpractice firms with a different reach

A malpractice firm that takes cases across a whole state, or one that practices in England and Wales, needs a different plan from a local injury firm. The two H3s below cover each.

### Statewide practices and the Map pack

A statewide malpractice practice still needs its Map listing, for searches near its offices and for its own name. Google's local ranking guide names three factors, [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), so a listing reaches only so far from the office.

Statewide reach comes from harm pages and state-rule pages instead. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) allow one profile per location and tell practitioners not to add profiles for each specialization, so a birth injury profile beside the main one is not an option.

### Clinical negligence firms in the UK

In England and Wales, the search terms are "clinical negligence" and "medical negligence", and directories such as ReviewSolicitors showed up in the results we checked.

[NHS Resolution](https://resolution.nhs.uk/about/), an arm's length body of the Department of Health and Social Care, handles clinical claims for members of its indemnity schemes.

Accreditation is a trust signal pages may name only when a lawyer holds it. [AvMA panel accreditation](https://avma.org.uk/resources-for-professionals/panel-accreditation/) is open to clinical negligence lawyers with at least six years' post-qualification experience, at firms in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

SRA guidance applies the [LASPO referral fee ban](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/prohibition-of-referral-fees-in-laspo-56-60/) to claims for damages after personal injury or death, from 1 April 2013. Check with the SRA how it applies to your clinical negligence work.

Clinical negligence is not on the SRA's [price-publishing list](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/) as of November 2026, so under those rules a fee page for that work is a choice, not a duty.

## Which agency habits turn good malpractice cases away?

Agencies turn good malpractice cases away when they run the injury playbook: chase volume, copy fee lines and ask only happy clients for reviews. Five habits do the most damage:

- **Chasing every "malpractice" search.** Searches about suing a lawyer or becoming one bring no clients. We rank for the medical searches only.
- **Copying injury fee lines.** "One-third" can break a state's malpractice cap. Our fee pages follow the rule where you practice.
- **Review requests to happy clients only.** Google bars selective requests. Our routine asks everyone the same way.
- **A page for every town.** City pages with swapped names are the [doorway pattern](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) Google describes. We build by harm and by state.
- **Counting calls, not cases you can take.** A ringing phone means little if most callers don't fit. We count qualified leads.

Medical malpractice SEO earns its keep when the pages screen as well as they rank, inside each state's rules.
