A client has to find you, believe you and reach you. Everything a firm does to win clients serves one of those three jobs. This guide sorts the usual channels by how well they do those jobs, and spends most of its time on search, because that is where we work and where many new matters begin.

I have no study to quote on how many clients come from each channel, and the answer differs by practice area and city. Use this as a way to check your own firm: ask every new client how they found you, and write it down.

## The path a client takes

Almost nobody goes from "I need a lawyer" to a signed engagement in one step. The usual path looks like this.

1. They have a problem and search for it in plain words.
2. They look at a few results, mostly the map pack and the first organic listings.
3. They check reviews, then your website.
4. They call, or send a form, and sometimes do both with two firms.

A referral skips step one and goes straight to step three. A friend gave them your name, and they still look you up. So even your referral business depends on what Google shows when someone types your firm name.

## Questions to ask every new client

A short, steady intake habit tells you more than any industry report. Ask each new client:

- What did you type or say when you first looked for help?
- Which results did you open, and which firms did you compare?
- What made you call us rather than someone else?
- Did anyone recommend us, and did you look us up afterward?

Write the answers in one place. After three months you will see which searches, listings and referral sources actually produce your clients, and you can put your effort there.

## Search: the main channel for most firms

### Organic results

Organic results are the standard blue links. They reward pages that match what the person typed, load well on a phone, and have earned links and mentions from elsewhere. Google reads the phone version of your site first ([mobile-first indexing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing)). If your site is awkward on a phone, that is the version being judged.

What to do:

- Give each practice area and each office its own page, written around the words clients use.
- Put the phone number and a short form where a thumb can reach them.
- Answer the questions that come before hiring: cost, timing, what happens next.

### Google Maps and the local pack

For "near me" and city searches, the map pack often sits above everything else. Google ranks local results on three things: how well a listing matches the search, how close it is to the searcher, and how well known it is. Review count and links feed that last one, per Google's own [local ranking help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).

You control relevance and part of prominence. Fill in every detail on your Google Business Profile, pick accurate categories, keep hours true, and use your real firm name. Google's [name guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) do not allow taglines or added service and location details in it. You cannot control distance, so do not chase searches from the far side of a metro area.

### Reviews

Reviews do two things at once. They help you rank in Maps, and they decide whether a person who has found you calls. Google lets you ask clients for a review with a link or QR code and bans rewards in return ([review help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

A routine works better than a campaign:

- Ask every client at the same point in the matter, such as when it closes.
- Send the direct link by text or email.
- Answer every review in a calm, short note, with no case details.

Many bar rules limit how you describe results and what you say about clients. Check your state's rules before you quote a review on your site.

### AI answers

People now ask AI tools questions like "who handles workplace retaliation claims near me." Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in its AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond normal search practice. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet ([AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

That means the work is the same as for search: clear pages, accurate listings, consistent facts about your firm everywhere it appears. We check what AI answers say about a firm's main searches each month, because it shows gaps that ordinary rank tracking misses. No one can promise that your firm will be named.

## How to start with search

If you do nothing else, do these in order.

1. **Search your own firm name.** Is your site first? Is the Maps listing right? Are there duplicate or old profiles?
2. **Search your main service plus your city** on a phone. Note who appears and what their result says.
3. **Fix your Google Business Profile.** Categories, hours, phone, services, photos.
4. **Check your top three pages.** Do they load fast, state the service and city, and show a way to call?
5. **Start the review routine.**
6. **Add one page for the service you most want more of.**

Search takes months to build, and anyone who promises a date is guessing. What you can promise yourself is that every month you add something concrete.

## Other channels, briefly and honestly

### Referrals

Referrals are often the best clients, and most firms already get some. They grow when you stay in touch with past clients and with lawyers in nearby practice areas. Remember that referred clients still search for you, so keep your listings accurate. Check your state's rules before giving or accepting anything of value for a referral.

### Networking and speaking

Bar events, chamber of commerce meetings and talks to community groups build trust and referrals slowly. They suit lawyers who enjoy them. They are poor value if you attend out of duty and never follow up.

### Directories

Listings on legal directories help in two ways: they are another place a person may find you, and they are a check that your details match. Keep the firm name, address and phone number identical everywhere. Skip directories that charge a lot for a link and show you no calls.

### Social media

Social media helps people remember you and gives you something to share. It rarely brings a client who has never heard of you. Our guide to [social media post ideas](/law-firm-social-media-post-ideas/) shows how to make posts support search and reviews.

### Free consultations

A free first meeting lowers the barrier to calling, and some practice areas expect it. Say plainly on the page how long it lasts and what it covers, so a call does not turn into free advice. Decide at the start who answers, because an unanswered call is a lost client.

### Webinars and workshops

These work for estate, business and immigration firms that teach a defined group. They need preparation and follow-up. Treat a recording as page content, with a written summary, so it keeps working after the event.

## Work that pays off slowly

Two things build over years, not weeks. The first is a body of pages that answer real questions in your area. The second is a pile of honest reviews. A new firm cannot buy either, but a firm that adds a little each month ends up far ahead of one that does a burst of work and stops.

Be wary of promises that skip this. Fast rankings do happen, but they rest on groundwork. A truck accident page we published for a PI firm in New Orleans ranked #1 within hours, after about three months of foundation work.

## Make the call easy to win

Visibility does nothing if the call goes unanswered or the intake is slow. Four checks:

- **Who answers, and when.** If nobody can answer at night, say so on the page and offer a form.
- **How fast you respond.** Test it. Call your own number and submit your own form.
- **What the caller hears.** Greet them by the firm name as it appears online.
- **Where the lead is recorded.** Note the source of each lead, so you know which channel earns its keep.

## Where to focus

Be honest about your capacity too. A plan you can keep up for a year beats a bigger plan you drop in March.

Pick the two or three channels that match your practice, and do them well. For most firms that means search and Maps, a review routine, and good referral habits. Add others when those are running. Spreading across ten channels thinly is the usual way firms burn budget with nothing to show.
