There is no single trick that fills a personal injury practice. There is a sequence of jobs that, done in order and kept up, make a firm easy to find, easy to trust and easy to call. This is the sequence I use. It is built around search, because that is where an injured person, or a family member, often starts.

I will not give you a timeline or a lead number. Both depend on your city, your competitors and where your site is today. What I can give you is the order of work and how to tell whether each step is done.

## Step 1: Decide which cases you want

Every later choice depends on this one. Write down:

- The case types you want more of, such as car, trucking, motorcycle, wrongful death or premises claims.
- The ones you accept only sometimes.
- The ones you refer out.
- The places where you will take cases, by city and county.
- The minimum case you will take, so staff can screen calls.

A firm that wants every case ranks for none of them. A firm that says "serious injury and trucking cases in three counties" can build pages and a profile that say exactly that.

## Step 2: Take a baseline

Before you change anything, record where you stand. You can do this in an afternoon.

- Search your firm name and your top three case types in your city, on a phone. Screenshot the results.
- Note which firms appear in the map results and what their listings show.
- Open Search Console and save the top queries and pages for the last three months.
- Count calls from your Google Business Profile for a recent month.
- Ask your intake staff how many callers last month were real cases.

This record is how you will know later whether anything worked.

## Step 3: Make the profile and the facts match

Local results come from relevance, distance and prominence, per [Google's help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). The first thing to fix is relevance, because it is cheap.

- Use your real firm name and nothing else. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) do not allow taglines or added service and location details.
- Choose a primary category that fits the main work and add secondary ones that are true.
- List the services you provide.
- Put the same name, address and phone number on your site, your bar listing and your directories.
- Add current photos of the office and the team.
- Give each real office its own profile.

Inconsistent facts confuse Google and people, and they confuse AI tools that copy from several sources.

## Step 4: Build pages in the right order

Most firms write pages in a random order. Use this one.

1. **The case type that pays best, with your city.** One strong page, not five thin ones.
2. **The question that comes before hiring.** What does a lawyer cost, and what happens after the first call?
3. **The next two case types.**
4. **Location pages for each real office.**
5. **Process pages.** What to do after a crash, how long claims take, what to say to an adjuster.
6. **Answers to the questions clients ask you.**

Each page should name the situation, explain the process in plain words, show who handles it and give a clear way to call. Do not promise results. Do not copy another firm's page with the city swapped.

Open every page on a phone. The phone version is what Google reads first ([mobile-first indexing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing)), and its [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) set a target of 2.5 seconds or less for the main content to load.

## What a strong case-type page contains

Step 4 asks for a strong page for each case type. Here is what goes on one, using a trucking claim as the example.

- **The situation in the reader's words.** "Hit by a semi truck" is closer to what people type than "commercial motor vehicle collision."
- **What is different about this kind of claim.** More than one party may be responsible, such as the driver, the carrier or a maintenance company. Records may need to be requested early. Explain this plainly.
- **The first steps.** Medical care, photos, names, what not to sign.
- **How a claim moves.** Investigation, treatment, demand, negotiation, filing. Say that timing varies.
- **Who will handle it.** Name the lawyer, with a short bio and a link.
- **Costs.** Explain that most injury work is paid as a share of any recovery, and link to your fee page.
- **How to reach you.** Number, form and what happens next.

Skip claims such as "we get the most money." A visitor cannot check them, and advertising rules in many states restrict them.

## Common reasons the system stalls

- The case list in Step 1 was never written, so each page tries to cover everything.
- The profile was fixed but the site still lacks pages for the main cases.
- Pages were published, but nobody answers forms the same day.
- Reviews are requested in bursts and then dropped.
- No one checks the numbers, so nobody notices when something breaks.

If one of these sounds familiar, return to that step first.

## Step 5: Fix the first call

Search can send people to you. It cannot make anyone stay on the phone. Most losses happen here.

- Test your own number at different times of day. Who answers, and how long does it take?
- Write a short intake script that covers the incident, injuries, treatment and other lawyers.
- Decide how after-hours calls and forms get answered, and say so on the site.
- Respond to web forms in minutes, not the next morning.
- Record the source and outcome of every lead.

A firm with strong rankings and slow intake gives its cases to the firm that picks up.

## Step 6: Earn reviews and links

Reviews and links build prominence. Google lets you ask clients to leave a review with a link, and bans paying or rewarding them for it ([review help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

For reviews:

- Ask at a set point in each case, such as when it settles.
- Send the link by text.
- Reply to each review briefly, without case details.

For links, look for things you already do:

- Local bar and trial lawyer association directories.
- Safety groups, medical and community organizations you work with.
- Local press, when you offer a useful comment on a law change or a local issue.
- Sponsorships you already pay for.

Skip bought links. They carry risk and bring no clients.

## Step 7: Check how AI answers describe you

Google's [AI features guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page needs no special markup to appear in its AI answers, only to be indexed and eligible for a snippet. Once a month, ask AI tools the questions your clients ask. Note which firms they name, which sources they use and whether any detail about your firm is wrong. Correct the sources, not the AI.

## Step 8: Run a monthly loop

This is the step that keeps the other seven working.

A system needs a rhythm. Each month:

1. Compare the baseline with this month: rankings for your core searches, profile calls, form entries.
2. Look at the pages that gained or lost clicks in Search Console.
3. Pick the next fixes: a page to improve, a profile detail to update, a review to answer.
4. Review the intake notes. Which leads were real cases, and which pages did they come from?
5. Write down what you changed and why, so next month you can tell what worked.

Small, steady changes add up. Keep the notes in one shared file so a new staff member can pick up where the last one stopped. Large rebuilds done once and left alone do not.

## A note on small firms

A solo lawyer or a two-lawyer firm cannot do everything at once, and does not need to. Pick one case type and one city, finish Steps 1 to 3 for them, and publish a single strong page. Add the next case type only when calls from the first are being answered and recorded. Narrow work done well beats broad work done thinly, and it is easier to keep up when you are also trying cases.

## What to expect and what not to

- Results build over months, and nobody can promise a date or a position.
- Competitive cities take longer.
- A strong page can help quickly, but it will not hold without reviews, links and a good profile.
- Advertising rules apply to every page. Have a lawyer in the firm approve each one.
