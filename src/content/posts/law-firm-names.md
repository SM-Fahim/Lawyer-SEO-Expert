A law firm name is a search asset before it is a brand. People type it into Google. Maps prints it above your reviews. AI tools repeat it when they list firms. If the name is hard to spell, shared with three other firms, or stuffed with keywords, you pay for that every month.

This guide covers how a name behaves in search, the rules to check, and a large set of ideas grouped by how each kind of name will work for you. The ideas are examples with made-up surnames. Check every one against your state bar, the trademark database and Google before you commit.

## What a name does in search

Three things happen to your name once it goes public.

- **People search it directly.** These are your branded searches. A clear, unique name means the first result is your own site, not a directory.
- **It sits on your Maps listing.** Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence reflects links and reviews ([Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). A name people remember is a name people review and link to.
- **It gets copied.** AI answers and directories repeat your name as written. If three sites spell it three ways, the mentions do not add up.

## Rules to check before you fall for a name

### How Google treats the name on your profile

Google asks that your profile name reflect the real-world name on your signs, website and stationery. Its [name guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) rule out taglines, phone numbers, web addresses, service descriptors and location add-ons. Legal suffixes such as LLC are allowed only if you can show they are part of the real name.

In plain words: "Harlan & Pike" is fine. "Harlan & Pike Best Car Accident Lawyer Dallas" is not, and profiles like it risk suspension. If your registered name is already a keyword string, expect this problem on every listing.

### Keyword stuffing

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe keyword stuffing as filling a page with keywords to manipulate rankings, including blocks of city names and repeated awkward phrases. A firm called "Houston Truck Accident Injury Lawyers Group" has that problem built in, because the name itself is the keyword string and repeats on every page. Use descriptive words only when they read like a name a person would say aloud.

### Bar rules, in plain words

The American Bar Association's model rules treat a firm name as a communication about your services, so it cannot mislead (Rule 7.1 and its comments; see [Minnesota's copy](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/court_rules/rule/prcond-7.1/pdf)). Trouble usually comes from names that suggest a link to a government body, a lawyer who is not with the firm, a charity, or a result you cannot back up. The ABA's [Litigation Journal summary](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/litigation-journal/2022-spring/ethical-limits-firm-names) walks through the main limits.

States differ. Some restrict trade names, some ask for a licensed lawyer's surname, and some regulate words like "associates" or "group" when they suggest more lawyers than you have. Read your state bar's rule on firm names. If the name is borderline, ask the bar's ethics line before you print anything.

### Trademark and entity checks

Search the USPTO trademark database, your state business registry and the state bar directory. Then search the name in quotes on Google. If another firm already owns that results page, you will be fighting for your own name for as long as the firm exists.

## A quick test for any name

Run each candidate through six questions.

1. Can someone spell it after hearing it once?
2. Does a Google search for it show no other firm on page one?
3. Is the .com, or a clean alternative, free? The [domain names post](/lawyer-domain-names/) covers this.
4. Are the social handles free?
5. Will it still fit if you add a practice area or a second office?
6. Can you say it on a phone greeting without explaining it?

Failing question two costs the most. You cannot fix it with content.

## How AI answers handle a firm name

When someone asks an AI tool for a family lawyer in your city, the answer is built from pages and listings that name firms. Three habits help your name travel cleanly through those answers.

- Use one exact spelling, including "&" or "and", on every page and listing.
- State the name, city and practice together in plain sentences on your home page and About page.
- Keep your address and phone number identical on every directory.

A distinctive name helps here too. An invented or uncommon name is easy for a system to tie to one firm. A generic one gets mixed up with others.

## Surname names

The classic form still works, and for search it has one advantage: an uncommon surname is rarely duplicated.

- Okafor Law Group
- Lindqvist & Moreau
- Brennan Tran Law
- Castellano Legal
- Whitlock Rhee PLLC
- Abara Law Office
- Delgado & Finch
- Marchetti Law Firm
- Kowalczyk Legal Counsel
- Yamamoto & Hale

The risk is a common surname. Smith, Johnson or Garcia will share results with dozens of firms. Add a first name, or pick another form.

Long strings of surnames also get shortened by clients in conversation. The short version is what people will search, so choose a name whose first two names work alone.

## Surname plus place

A place word helps people find you and helps Google understand where you work. It works when the place is real and stable.

- Okafor Law of Tulsa
- Brennan Tran, Savannah
- Delgado Finch of the Rio Grande Valley
- Castellano Legal, Hudson Valley
- Marchetti Law, North Shore
- Whitlock Rhee, Piedmont

Keep the place out of your Google Business Profile name unless it is part of the name on your door. Put it in the page title, the address and the description instead.

If you may open offices elsewhere, pick a region word that can stretch, such as "Gulf Coast" or "Mountain West", rather than a single town.

## Names that describe the work

Descriptive names are easy to understand and hard to own. They suit a firm built around one niche.

- Harbor Injury Counsel
- Clear Title Law
- Second Chance Defense
- Open Door Immigration Law
- Steady Hand Estate Planning
- Fair Share Divorce Lawyers
- Rail & Road Injury Law
- Clean Slate Record Relief
- Final Wishes Legal
- Small Business Counsel Co.

Two warnings. First, some bars treat words like "specialist" or "expert" as a claim that needs a certification. Second, a descriptive phrase may already be in use by many firms, which weakens your branded search.

## Coined and invented names

An invented word is the easiest name to own in search, because nobody else has it. It takes more work to explain, so pair it with a plain descriptor in your page titles and homepage heading.

- Verdara Law
- Lexion Partners
- Tallyr Legal
- Brevity Law
- Corvane Counsel
- Navis Legal Group
- Oakfold Law
- Plainwell Legal
- Sentry & Vale
- Quillmark Law

Say the name out loud and spell it to a friend. If they get it wrong twice, it will be misspelled in searches too.

## Latin and classical names

Latin reads as traditional and formal. It suits estate, business and appellate firms. It is also crowded, so pick unusual combinations and check each one in search.

- Veritas Harbor Law
- Aequitas Counsel
- Fidelis Legal Group
- Lumen Juris
- Pax Legal
- Sapiens & Ward
- Mens Rea Defense
- Terra Firma Real Estate Law
- Ad Astra Legal
- Lex Fidei Partners

Do not add Latin only because it sounds serious. A client who has never hired a lawyer may not know how to say it, and a name people cannot say is a name they cannot refer.

## Names built from a theme

Some firms pick a word that stands for how they work, then add a plain descriptor. Themes that read well in this field:

- **Water and weather:** Tidewater Law, Clearing Skies Legal, Highwater Counsel.
- **Trees and land:** Cedar Row Law, Oak & Ember Legal, Homestead Counsel.
- **Paths and travel:** Trailhead Law, Waypoint Legal, Long Road Counsel.
- **Craft and building:** Cornerstone Legal Group, Mason & Reed Law, Plumb Line Counsel.
- **Light and sight:** Lamplight Law, Clear Sight Legal, Daybreak Counsel.

Whichever theme you pick, avoid words that promise an outcome, such as "Victory" or "Justice Served". Bar rules on unjustified expectations apply to names as well as ads.

## Modern short names

Short names fit a phone screen, a billboard and a social handle. They suit firms that want a fresher feel.

- Anchor Legal
- Meridian Law
- Ledger Legal
- Northbound Law
- Pivot Law
- Keystone Counsel
- Fieldstone Legal
- Beacon & Barr
- Juniper Law
- Hollis Law

The trade-off is that single dictionary words are common. "Anchor Law" will exist in several states. Search each name with "attorney" after it and see who shows up.

## Names for solo practice

A solo lawyer has one big choice: use your own name or a firm name. Your own name is honest, easy to defend under bar rules and simple to verify. A firm name makes it easier to grow or sell later.

Forms that work for one lawyer today and more later:

- [Surname] Law, PLLC
- Law Office of [First Last]
- [Surname] Legal Counsel
- [Surname] & Co. Law
- The [Surname] Firm
- [Place] Family Law Practice
- Lantern Law
- Rowan Legal
- Bridgewater Counsel
- Sparrow Law Office

If you use a name that is not your own, check your state's rule on trade names. Make sure your website says plainly who the lawyer is.

## How to pick the final name

Take your top five and do this.

1. Search each name in quotes on Google and in Google Maps.
2. Check the trademark database and your state registry.
3. Check domain and handle availability.
4. Read your state bar's firm name rule.
5. Say the name in a phone greeting and ask three people to write it down.

Keep the one that passes every check, even if it is not your favorite.

## After you choose

Use the name the same way everywhere: website, Google Business Profile, bar directory, Avvo, Yelp, social handles and email signature. Mismatched spellings split your reviews and mentions between listings.

If you are renaming an existing firm, keep the old name visible for a while ("formerly known as") and redirect the old domain properly. The steps are in our post on [lawyer domain names](/lawyer-domain-names/).
